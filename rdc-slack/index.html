#security on Slack

Where to reach the security team, how our channels are named, and the few rules that keep them useful.

Last updated 8 October 2026. More channels will be added and announced in #security-team.

We work in the open.

Security's Jira projects, Confluence spaces and Slack channels are open by default, incident channels included. The exceptions are team channels, the threat intel channel, automated alert feeds, restricted incidents and vendor channels. They are private because of what they contain.

How channels are named

Four patterns cover every security channel. The prefix tells you what kind of channel it is before you open it.

#security-team

Standing channels: the company-facing front door, the policy library and one private channel per security team.

Examples: #security, #security-policies, #security-cloudsec
#sec-topic

Projects and topics that need a dedicated space, such as a compliance framework or a time-bound initiative. Public by default.

Examples: #sec-nis2, #sec-gdpr, #sec-project-abc
#sec-alerts-team

Automated alerts and updates from our security tooling, one channel per owning team. Bots post, people triage in threads.

Examples: #sec-alerts-itsec, #sec-alerts-cloudsec
#secinc-ticket

One channel per security incident, named after its ticket in the SECINC Jira project. Slack only accepts lowercase, so SECINC-42 becomes #secinc-42.

Examples: #secinc-42, #secinc-43-r for a restricted incident

Working rules

Short on purpose. If something here does not fit a real situation, raise it in #security-team and we will update this page.

Asking the security team

  • Ask quick questions in #security. Anyone in the company can post there.
  • If a request will take us more than 5 minutes, we will ask you to open a SECHELP ticket. Tickets get tracked, prioritised and answered by whoever is on shift.
  • Tag @security-oncall for anything urgent. Avoid direct messages to individual team members for requests.
  • Announcements, advisories and required actions are also posted in #security.
  • Find security policies, procedures, SOPs and resources in #security-policies. It is read-only; the pinned post links to everything.

Automated alert channels

  • sec-alerts-* channels carry tool output only. Bots post; people reply in threads or move the work into a ticket.
  • They are private because alert payloads contain usernames, IP addresses and detection logic.
  • Each alert channel belongs to the team named in its suffix. That team owns triage and noise tuning.

Incidents

  • Every incident registered in the SECINC Jira project gets its own channel: #secinc- followed by the ticket number.
  • The channel topic holds the Jira link, current severity and the incident commander.
  • Each new incident channel is announced automatically in #security-incidents, along with severity changes, closure and the postmortem link.
  • Incident channels are public by default. Join to follow along; keep discussion to responders unless you are asked to contribute.
  • Never switch a public incident channel to private mid-incident. Anyone who already joined keeps what they read. Open a new -r channel and move the conversation there.
  • If Slack itself may be compromised, move to the out-of-band channel defined in the incident response plan.
  • Incident channels are archived within 2 weeks of the postmortem.

Create a restricted channel (#secinc-NN-r, private from the start) when the incident involves:

  • a specific employee, contractor or insider
  • HR, Legal or law enforcement
  • personal or health data of identifiable people
  • suspected compromise of Slack, email or the identity provider

Topic and project channels

  • Use #sec-project-name for a project that needs its own space and #sec-topic for a standing subject such as a compliance framework.
  • These are public by default.
  • Check with a security lead before creating one, to avoid duplicates.
  • Archive the channel when the project closes or after 90 days without activity.

Housekeeping

  • Channel names are lowercase, with hyphens between words and no spaces.
  • Every channel has a one-line purpose that says who it is for.
  • Team channels are private. Everything else is public unless this page lists it as private.
  • Vendor channels use Slack Connect and are named #sec-ext-vendor.
